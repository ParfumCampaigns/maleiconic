Stone Eby is an American college football tight end from Flower Mound, Texas, who played five seasons for the SMU Mustangs (2021–2025) and became internationally recognized in July 2026 when he proposed to Emma Gretzky, the youngest daughter of Wayne Gretzky — the greatest hockey player who ever lived. The engagement, announced via Instagram on July 29, 2026, made Eby the future son-in-law of “The Great One” and the future brother-in-law of PGA Tour champion Dustin Johnson, who married Emma’s older sister Paulina Gretzky in 2022. The couple has a son, Walter Boone-Gretzky Eby, born August 11, 2025 — Wayne Gretzky’s sixth grandchild, named for Wayne’s late father Walter Gretzky, the man who built the backyard rink in Brantford, Ontario, where the greatest career in hockey history began.[1][2]

Early life and high school

Eby grew up in Flower Mound, Texas, a suburb northwest of Dallas in Denton County. He attended Flower Mound High School, where he was a three-star recruit rated by 247Sports, Rivals, and ESPN. As a prep defensive end, Eby recorded 78 tackles, 22.5 tackles for loss, and six sacks as a junior, and 58 tackles, 14 tackles for loss, and 3.5 sacks as a senior. He added six pass breakups, two forced fumbles, and three recoveries. He helped Flower Mound to a bi-district championship in 2019, a district championship in 2018, and a second-round appearance in the Texas 6A Division I playoffs. He held offers from Iowa State, Baylor, Purdue, Georgia Tech, and Washington State, among others, before committing to SMU.[3]

College football career

Eby enrolled at Southern Methodist University in 2021 as a defensive tackle wearing number 47. Over five seasons, he underwent one of the more unusual position transformations in recent SMU history: from defensive tackle (2021–2022) to fullback (2023) to tight end (2024–2025), switching from #47 to #44 along the way. A 6-foot-5, 260-pound athlete who began his career rushing the quarterback and ended it catching passes from one, Eby appeared in 44 games across five seasons.[3]

In 2023, playing as a fullback, he appeared in all 14 games and recorded his first career reception and touchdown at Oklahoma. He was named a finalist for the 2023 Lowman Trophy Award, given annually to the nation’s top fullback. In 2024, now playing tight end, he appeared in all 14 games and recorded a reception for six yards against Penn State in the first round of the College Football Playoff — SMU’s first CFP appearance following its move to the ACC. In 2025, he appeared in all 13 games and scored his first career rushing touchdown in the final game of his college career, a win against Arizona in the Trust & Will Holiday Bowl on January 2, 2026.[3]

Engagement to Emma Gretzky

Stone Eby and Emma Gretzky met at SMU. Emma, the youngest of Wayne and Janet Jones Gretzky’s five children, attended SMU on an athletic scholarship and played on the Mustangs’ tennis team for her first two years before graduating in May 2025. Eby and Emma began dating in November 2022.[1][4]

On August 11, 2025, Emma gave birth to their son, Walter Boone-Gretzky Eby — Wayne Gretzky’s sixth grandchild. The baby’s first name honors Wayne’s father, Walter Gretzky (1938–2021), the Bell Canada repairman who built the backyard rink in Brantford, Ontario, that produced the most prolific scorer in hockey history. Walter Gretzky was appointed to the Order of Canada in 2007 for his philanthropic work and died in March 2021.[2][5]

On July 29, 2026, Eby proposed to Emma at a ranch. The couple shared the news on Instagram with the caption “The easiest yes to a lifetime with you.” The engagement photos featured Eby in jeans, a button-up shirt, and a cowboy hat, Emma in a floral sundress, and their son Walter in a miniature cowboy hat of his own. Janet Jones Gretzky commented “Congratulations Love You All !” and Paulina Gretzky wrote “I’m crying!! no one deserves this more.”[1]

The Gretzky dynasty

The engagement places Stone Eby inside one of the most prominent families in the history of professional sports and celebrity culture. His future father-in-law, Wayne Gretzky, holds or shares 61 NHL records, including career goals (894), career assists (1,963), and career points (2,857) — numbers so dominant that the NHL retired his number 99 league-wide. Wayne won four Stanley Cup championships with the Edmonton Oilers, nine Hart Trophies as league MVP, and ten Art Ross Trophies as scoring champion. He is universally regarded as the greatest ice hockey player of all time.[6]

His future mother-in-law, Janet Jones Gretzky, is an actress and model who appeared in A Chorus Line (1985), The Flamingo Kid, and Police Academy 5. She met Wayne at a Los Angeles Lakers game in 1987. They have been married for 38 years.[7]

His future sister-in-law, Paulina Gretzky, is a model, singer, and social media personality who married two-time major champion Dustin Johnson in 2022. Johnson, a 24-time PGA Tour winner and 2020 Masters champion, shares two sons with Paulina: Tatum (born 2015) and River (born 2017). Emma’s brothers include Ty Gretzky (co-founder of The Gretzky Hockey School with Wayne), Trevor Gretzky (former minor league baseball player who played at San Diego State under Hall of Famer Tony Gwynn), and Tristan Gretzky (golfer who played at Pepperdine and NYU).[8]

Wayne and Janet’s six grandchildren span the Gretzky-Johnson, Gretzky-Gretzky, and now Gretzky-Eby families: Tatum and River (Paulina and Dustin Johnson), Sicily and Vienna (Ty and Sara Gretzky), Phoenix (Trevor and Ashley Gretzky), and Walter (Emma and Stone Eby).[2]

Legacy

Stone Eby was never a star. His college stat line over five seasons at SMU adds up to a handful of receptions and two touchdowns. He started his career as a defensive tackle, was converted to fullback, then to tight end, and finished with a rushing touchdown in a bowl game that most people outside of Dallas didn’t watch. He held no major individual awards. He was not drafted. He will not play on Sundays.

What he did was propose to a girl he met at school — who happened to be Wayne Gretzky’s daughter. He named their son after the man who built the rink. And on a ranch somewhere in late July, wearing a cowboy hat next to a woman in a sundress and a baby in a miniature version of the same hat, he became part of the most storied family in the history of hockey.
